Abstract:
STREAM3R reformulates pointmap prediction as a decoder-only Transformer problem. It targets scalable sequential 3D reconstruction from image streams by using causal attention, cached context from prior frames, and LLM-style training/inference infrastructure. The paper states that the model predicts dense 3D geometry for incoming frames, generalizes across static and dynamic scene benchmarks, and supports online 3D perception in streaming environments.

Relevant extracted technical details:
- The paper frames streaming visual input as a setting where applications continuously process new observations and update reconstruction on the fly.
- It contrasts STREAM3R with fixed-set multi-view methods that re-run full reconstruction when a new image arrives, and with memory/RNN-style methods that can drift or have limited memory.
- The method processes incoming frames sequentially with causal attention over accumulated observations and cached features from past frames.
- Outputs include local-coordinate point maps, global-coordinate point maps, and camera pose/intrinsic information.
- The problem formulation maps a stream of RGB images to per-frame 3D attributes, including local and global point maps and camera pose.
- The related-work discussion identifies pointmap-based representations as a feed-forward dense 3D geometry format and argues that prior pairwise or global-attention approaches have scalability issues.
